# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Church volunteers and staff prepare and operate worship services, often with little or no production training. The main computer is always sufficient to run a service; optional colleagues can operate delegated areas from connected devices. Users may be working under time pressure on Sunday, with equipment that can disconnect and without Internet access.

## Product Purpose

Rhema Studio is a local-first platform for planning church services, preparing reusable worship content and designs, presenting live sources, and routing independent congregation, stage, signage, and online productions. A successful experience lets a volunteer understand what is planned, what is live, and what an action will affect before taking it. The main computer remains capable of the complete production when nobody else joins.

## Brand Personality

Calm, dependable, welcoming. The interface should help volunteers feel oriented and in control while giving experienced operators precise tools when needed.

## Anti-references

- Dense broadcast-engineer dashboards that expose transport jargon before a volunteer can choose a screen.
- Setup flows that require a volunteer to understand NDI, HTTP, routing, or certificates just to configure a common display.
- Controls that hide whether an edit is still a draft, will be saved for later, or will change an active output immediately.
- Per-verse destination checklists, duplicate operator consoles, and other flows that make routine service operation depend on extra staff.

## Design Principles

- Keep the solo main-machine workflow complete; connected volunteers add optional, scoped controls.
- Show the next useful choice in familiar language, and reveal specialist settings when the task calls for them.
- Keep Plan, Live, and History distinct; preview edits privately and make their effect on outputs explicit.
- Publish each live source once and let configured feeds, scenes, and destinations decide how to show it.
- Explain failures and recovery in the place where they occur, without hiding whether an action succeeded.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Follow the project's WCAG AA contrast target. Make primary workflows usable by keyboard and screen readers, with visible focus, meaningful labels, and status that is not conveyed by color alone. Keep layouts usable at the supported desktop window sizes and respect reduced-motion preferences.
